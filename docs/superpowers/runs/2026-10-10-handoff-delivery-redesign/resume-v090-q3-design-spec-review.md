## Required revisions before tests-only authoring

### R1 — Scope the quiet-retry health assertion to the new recheck mechanism

Design lines 71 and 95 require repeated no-op/retained replies to produce no health update. That blanket assertion conflicts with existing deliberate mod ACK behavior: `src/service/dispatch.rs:908` calls `record_ack` whenever the typed report contains a result whose `counts_as_mod_ack()` is true; `src/protocol/watch.rs` defines both `Settled` and `AlreadySettled` as counting. This is separate from the fresh explicit presentation-confirmation health rule in TRUST-POLICY.md. The proposed receipt hint must not silently redefine that older ACK policy.

Revise the requirement to say that the **new receipt-recheck notification and its resulting passive pass/pull** add no health evidence, logical allocation, receipt evidence or duplicate model output. Existing canonical AckModDelivered accounting remains its own behavior. Tests should snapshot the health timestamp after the ACK route has returned and then prove that hint consumption and duplicate passive drains leave it unchanged. An ordinary Ack/retained-retry control can additionally prove an entirely unchanged health timestamp, since that path has no independent mod health action. Keep logical retry time fixed/below its due threshold when asserting no new output; a legitimately due offer is not a duplicate caused by the hint.

If the intended change really is to suppress existing AlreadySettled mod health accounting, that is an additional behavioral change requiring an explicit policy decision and causal test, rather than an incidental Q3 assertion.

### R2 — Account for the unbounded caller of `read_cursors::advance`

Design line 65 says per-moved-cursor invalidation is bounded by the existing input batch limit. That is not true of every caller of the proposed shared insertion point. `src/store/first_contact.rs:228` documents fast-forward for top-level and subagent bindings. Its implementation enumerates all joined-thread gaps and calls `read_cursors::advance` for **every gap** at line 286. `MAX_THREADS` bounds the returned descriptions, not that mutation loop. Adding the canonical lookup and up to two consumer reads/updates inside every `advance` introduces repeated private maintenance proportional to those gaps.

Specify one coalesced invalidation per affected binding at the owning fast-forward operation, or another concrete arrangement that preserves the claimed bound for added private maintenance. Add that owner to the implementation map if needed. Preserve successful cursor advancement for bindings with no current presentation consumer rows, including legitimate subagent fast-forward: presentation admission cannot become a new prerequisite for those established cursor operations. Refusal/overflow in an applicable current consumer must still roll the entire surrounding canonical operation back.

Add a focused many-thread fast-forward control that distinguishes returned-description limits from actual moved cursors and counts the added private work. This does not require redesigning the pre-existing fast-forward scan; it does require avoiding a false constant/batch-bound claim for new work.
