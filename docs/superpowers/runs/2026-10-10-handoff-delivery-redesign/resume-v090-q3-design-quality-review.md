## Required revisions

### Q3-Q1 — Fence the final active push against replacement, not merely its earlier observation (high)

Design lines 39 and 73 require exact-channel application, but preserving the current check is insufficient. `src/service/mod_channels.rs:556` checks the channel while updating fingerprints, releases the registry lock, then the changed-fingerprint branch calls `notify_binding(binding, version, now)` at line 574. `notify_binding` at line 588 has no channel argument and accepts whichever Live entry now occupies the binding. A replacement in that gap receives the old owner's Attention and gets its `last_attention_push` advanced. With mixed mode replacements this also crosses the negotiated mode boundary. The passive branch already rechecks the channel immediately around push, illustrating the missing active fence.

**Required design change:** require the snapshotted channel id and mode at the final active/passive send and associated local bookkeeping. Either hold one lock through the exact-entry comparison and nonblocking sink push, or pass the expected identity to a helper that rechecks under that lock. Applying a result from owner C1 must neither push nor update C2. Atomically taking/restoring the receipt bit must follow the same identity rule. A store-read check by itself is not the final fence.

**Test:** deterministically pause after the first observation and before the active send, replace V2 with Legacy (also test reverse replacement), then resume. C2 receives no old Attention, old pending bit or old stall timestamp. Separately inject a receipt event while a pass is outside the lock: the event remains pending for the next pass even when the earlier snapshot sends successfully. Replacement while store work fails must not restore into C2.

### Q3-Q2 — Select a representable post-commit receipt classification (medium)

Design lines 69–71 describe ordinary receipt-change notifications selected from the typed successful result, with retained-result ambiguity explicitly accepted. There is another ambiguity: `src/store/receipts.rs:337–341` maps **both** `SettlePlan::Settle` (ordinary receipt) and `SettlePlan::Complete` (Lazy display completion) to `ModAckOutcome::Settled`. `delivery_confirm` maps the same result to `DeliveryConfirmOutcome::Settled` at lines 200–208. The command/result type alone cannot identify the promised ordinary receipt subset. AlreadySettled can additionally accompany this binding's first cursor advancement; it is not synonymous with a no-op.

**Required design choice:** either (a) explicitly permit a conservative, coalesced seat-local recheck for successful ordinary-or-Lazy delivery settlement/completion results, while distinguishing that hint from receipt evidence, or (b) carry a bounded internal committed ordinary-receipt delta from the canonical transaction. Do not add post-commit per-item classification queries or describe `Settled` as proof of an ordinary receipt change. Keep errors, all-refused batches and presentation confirmation excluded. If choosing (b), retained replay behavior and the additional production path/API must be in the implementation map.

**Test:** ordinary-only, Lazy-only, mixed successful/refused and all-refused AckModDelivered/DeliveryConfirm batches; retained successful operation; fresh AlreadySettled display that advances a cursor; and rollback after tentative settlement. Assert the selected policy's exact affected seat/entries and no durable hint on rollback. Verify the foreground writer turn is released before registry bookkeeping, without holding a registry lock across store work.

### Q3-Q3 — Qualify duplicate-reply quietness against existing logical retry eligibility (medium)

Design line 71 unconditionally requires repeated no-op/retained replies to cause no new model output or ledger allocation. That exceeds the proposed mechanism: a receipt hint triggers an ordinary V2 pull, and `src/service/delivery_presentation.rs:179–210` recalculates due offers and sets `retry_messages: true`. `src/delivery/ledger.rs:22–42` makes an existing eligible offer due again after 60 seconds, up to the existing three-offer limit. A retained successful reply for one message can therefore trigger a valid retry of a different still-pending message at that same seat. Its logical offer stamp can advance without any fabricated receipt or wake authority. The JS mod may deduplicate previously delivered messages, but that does not prevent the daemon's logical allocation.

**Required design change:** scope the quiet oracle to unchanged canonical/render scope **and unchanged retry eligibility**, normally a frozen clock after all current continuation is drained. Separately require a due-clock control to preserve ordinary DP5 retry behavior; the hint itself must never reset/increment stamps, refill owner replay, create model Attention or directly write channel health. If absolute silence even for legitimately due mail is intended, that is a new pull mode/API design, not accomplished by the current boolean hint.

**Test:** repeat retained/no-op results before the due threshold and assert no output, ledger delta or health change; cross the threshold with a distinct pending eligible message and compare to an otherwise identical explicit ordinary pull. Do not weaken existing retry semantics to satisfy a test phrased too broadly.

## Sink and progress contract to make explicit

`ModChannelSink::push` **already returns bool** (`src/ports.rs:3808–3810`). Concrete `ChannelSink` uses an **unbounded** Tokio mpsc queue; `false` means the receiver is gone (`src/daemon/transport/watch_connection.rs:29–37`). There is no representable queue-full/retryable-overflow result and no bounded-queue overflow control to write against this production transport. No API change is necessary merely to inspect success.

A true return proves enqueue, not delivery or flush. Transport write failure unregisters the owner and reconnect/initial Accepted drains recover; it cannot be reported synchronously as a failed push. A false result can include enqueue immediately followed by receiver closure, so retry is inherently at-least-once. State the intended distinction. For false push, same-owner bit restoration is representable, but the design should state whether it marks the worker dirty or relies on the existing safety pass and imminent unregister; do not busy-loop a permanently dead sink. Restoration must OR with new pending state, never overwrite a concurrent event. Do not consume the bit solely because the fingerprint snapshot was stored before a failed send.

The 64-entry bound covers registry hints/worklists, not queued transport frames and not retained durable binding history. Keep those accounting domains separate. Queue coalescing or bounded transport would be a separate scoped change; do not claim the proposed boolean creates a new transport-memory cap.

Phrase the invariant as no fabricated/new receipt settlement or native-wake/health authority, not a blanket promise that no completion RPC ever occurs. Keep busy-context and idle-no-wake controls.
