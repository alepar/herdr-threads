## Confirmed findings
- [Should-fix] D1 item 4 'Grace. At least RESTORED_PANE_GRACE_MS ... since the watch row was recorded'; AC4; D1 rationale '3, 4 and 5 make sure retirement cannot race ... an idle mod watch reclaiming the seat within about 30 s'; D2 'retirement follows within one capture cadence after the grace ends' — The 1 h grace is measured from when the watch row was recorded, not from when the closure was first observed, so a pane closed after the first hour (or near its end) retires its seat on the next capture with no window for a resumed session or idle mod watch to reclaim, contradicting the spec's claim that conditions 3-5 prevent that race.
  verdict: confirmed (reproduce ✓ / refute ✗-survived / ground ✓)
  evidence: All three seats quote D1.4 ("since the watch row was recorded"), D1.2 (only the current publication P must show A and T absent), D2 ("within one capture cadence after the grace ends") and AC4 ("the first pass after the grace does"). The watch is written by the first coherent pass after the Herdr restart. A closure at t0+2h followed by a resume in another pane meets D1.2 and D1.4 on the next pass (about 5 s), D1.3 holds because no claim has been kept yet, D1.5 holds because the seat is unchanged, so the seat retires before the session's C1 claim or its ~30 s mod-watch reclaim. The ground seat notes D3 stores only `recorded_at`, so the grace cannot be measured from closure as written. The D1 rationale, the Accepted limit ("for an hour while the restored pane ... is closed") and the CHANGELOG line ("has not reclaimed them within an hour") all state a guarantee the mechanism does not provide.
  fix-shape hint: Record the first-observed-absence time (a `first_absent_at` column or a second watch state set by the first pass that sees A and T gone) and measure the grace from it; or, if the watch-anchored grace is intended, drop the reclaim-window claim from the rationale and reword the Accepted limit, AC4 and CHANGELOG to say what is enforced.

- [Should-fix] D1 item 4; D3 'recorded_at'; AC5 — The grace counts persisted wall-clock time (recorded_at) and no clock source is stated, so a laptop suspend, clock step or daemon downtime longer than the grace can exhaust it while no session could run or claim, retiring the seat on the first pass after resume or restart.
  verdict: confirmed (reproduce ✓ / refute ✗-survived / ground ✓)
  evidence: Reproduce and ground seats both searched the spec for a clock source, monotonic time, suspend or downtime handling and found none. D1.4 measures from `recorded_at`; AC5 requires the value to survive a daemon restart, so it is a stored wall-clock timestamp. While the daemon is down or the machine is suspended no kept claim can be written, so D1.3 holds trivially; on the first coherent pass after resume D1.2 and D1.4 both hold and D2's full pass fires `RetireRestoredPane` before the ~30 s mod-watch reclaim the D1 rationale says it protects. The refute seat argues "an hour without a claim" is the stated semantics and the limit is accepted; the two confirming seats answer that the rationale's reclaim-window claim fails and the Accepted limits do not cover time in which no claim was possible.
  severity note: confirming seats split Should-fix / Nit; Should-fix kept because the profile names the laptop as the motivating environment and seat retirement as irreversible. Shares a root cause and a fix with the finding above.
  fix-shape hint: Name the clock, and either count only observed uptime (advance the grace per coherent pass) or require at least one grace-length, or a short fixed delay, of observed passes after a daemon start or resume before any `Closed` decision.

The D1.1 parenthesis still overstates what a retaken baseline proves.

## Rejected (with reason)
- D1.2 'shows neither target A nor terminal T anywhere' — Address reuse at A within one incarnation would block closure forever and keep the restore holds. Rejected 3/3. Ground seat fetched the Herdr v0.7.0 release note ("closed tab and pane ids no longer retarget later resources"), so a closed A stays absent within one incarnation. Refute and reproduce seats add that even with reuse the outcome is a missed retirement, which the spec's "Closures not observed are not retired" limit already covers, and dropping the "neither A" clause would retire on weaker evidence.
- D1 item 1 'an address seen in another incarnation, or never held, starts no watch'; Acceptance 6; Accepted limits 'Closures not observed' — Seats whose address was never shown held in the new incarnation never retire and keep the server's holds. Rejected 2/3 (reproduce seat CONFIRM Should-fix, dissent noted). Refute and ground seats quote the Accepted limit's rule, "a seat whose pane closed while no publication of that incarnation recorded a watch for it", with "for example" marking the two named cases as illustrations, and AC6's explicit "a target not in the baseline or released ... never retire"; retiring a never-observed pane would be the absent-address heuristic the Rejected list and AGENTS.md forbid. The dissent asks for the limit to name the never-restored case explicitly; that is a documentation point the majority's evidence already answers.
- D1 'The saved address only chooses which pane to watch; it is never the evidence'; Accepted limit 1 — The address link is the real evidence, so a seat alive elsewhere can retire. Rejected 3/3. The spec states the link openly (D1), names the cost in Accepted limit 1, and bounds it with D1.3 (kept claim), D1.4 (grace) and D1.5 (generation). The renumbering scenario needs Herdr to put another seat's pane at A and that seat's session to stay silent for an hour; the residual is the stated limit, and the next C1 claim goes through ordinary guards.
- D1 item 3; D1 closing paragraph '3, 4 and 5 make sure retirement cannot race C1 (a claim decided or kept)' — An in-flight or client-held C1 claim is invisible to condition 3. Rejected 3/3. The spec scopes its race protection to "a claim decided or kept"; a decided claim bumps the generation (AC3), a retryable refusal is kept, and a claim landing in the same transaction window is ordinary serialization whose loser goes through ordinary guards, which the Accepted limits name. A fence over client-side intent files would conflict with A2 (the daemon never reads them).
- D1 item 3 'any top-level binding of the seat' — Undefined for seats whose bindings all ended or carry no native session id. Rejected 3/3. "Any" is unqualified, so ended bindings count; the ground seat cites TRUST-POLICY.md line 133 (unresolved-seat matching over "any of its bindings, not only the latest"). A binding with no native session id has no kept claim to match, and conditions 1, 2, 4 and 5 still gate retirement.
- D2 'WatchRestoredPane upserts the row (one per seat)'; D3 table — No lifecycle for stale watch rows (old incarnation, bumped generation, reattach, operator action). Rejected 2/3 (ground seat CONFIRM Nit, dissent noted). D1.1 requires the row to match P's boot and incarnation and D1.5 the current generation, so a stale row is never decisive; "one row per seat" makes `Observe` overwrite it with a fresh `recorded_at`, which is the conservative direction. The dissent asks for the overwrite and cleanup rule to be stated in one sentence; worth doing alongside the grace fix, but not a design gap.
- D2 'one per seat'; D1 condition 1 — Several unresolved seats with the same saved target A all watch T and retire together. Rejected 3/3. D1 is a per-seat rule on the same structural fact; each seat still passes D1.3-D1.5 independently; a seat whose session is elsewhere and silent for an hour is the stated limit; no seat with a live binding or proof on T is affected.
- Accepted limits: 'the laptop seats from before this fix need a manual retire' — No backfill or one-shot path for already-unresolved legacy seats. Rejected 2/3 (reproduce seat CONFIRM Should-fix, dissent noted; it could not check the caller-requirement quote against the original ticket). A retroactive retire of a seat whose pane is already gone has no structural evidence and is the absent-address heuristic the spec rejects under C2 and AGENTS.md ("never end a binding on heuristic evidence"). Already-unresolved seats whose restored pane is still present are covered going forward by `Observe`. A listing of unresolved/other seats without a watch row would be a useful operator aid; suggestion, not a gap.
- D1 item 1; D2 'The applier re-derives the evidence inside its transaction'; AC5 — Unclear whether re-derivation checks the current (retaken) baseline or trusts the row. Rejected 3/3. D1.1 is past tense ("was written by a pass whose publication showed A ... while A was a member of that recovery's baseline"), so membership is a write-time condition; the Rejected bullet "Baseline membership only. The baseline is lost on the next daemon restart" is the reason the durable row exists; AC5 follows. The row is keyed by Herdr boot and incarnation, so no epoch column is needed.
- D1.1 'with P's boot and incarnation'; Problem para 2; AC5 — Whether the watch's boot changes on a daemon restart. Rejected 3/3. `host_boot` is the Herdr host boot (ground seat: `src/cli/topology_runtime.rs` sets `host_incarnation` from `observation.host_boot`; `src/host/native.rs` compares `incarnation.boot` with `preflight.host_boot`; the daemon's own `boot_id` lives in `src/daemon/lifecycle.rs`); reproduce seat: `src/store/seats.rs:1044` computes `new_recovery` as an OR over boot and epoch, so a restart trips it through epoch alone. AC5's "within the incarnation" makes the intent explicit. Terminology nit at most.
- D1 item 1 'with no release'; Acceptance 6 — Once A is released, the original seat can never watch or retire and blocks the hold lift. Rejected 3/3. AC6 deliberately excludes released targets; once another seat holds A or T the watched pane is in use and was not closed, so retiring S would be heuristic. The spec promises hold lift after a covered retirement (AC1), not that every unresolved seat self-retires; operator retire is unchanged (AC7).
- D1 condition 1 (write time) vs conditions 2-5 (decision time) — No rule invalidates the watch when A's hold is released after the row is written. Rejected 3/3. D1.3 is a decision-time check ("no other seat's structural proof or binding names T") and includes structural proof, which does not lapse with a binding; an operator rebind of the watched seat fails D1.5. The residual (S2 claims, retires, ended binding no longer names T) is the same hour-bounded class the Accepted limits name; a one-line clarification that ended bindings and historical proofs count would close the wording question.
- Accepted limits, 'Closures not observed are not retired'; D1 condition 2 — A pane closed after its watch but before a new incarnation, with no capture in between, is never retired. Rejected 2/3 (reproduce seat CONFIRM Nit, dissent noted). The limit's heading is general ("closures not observed"), the body's two cases are introduced by "for example", and treating A's absence from the new baseline as closure would reintroduce the cross-incarnation heuristic. The dissent asks for the example list to be widened by one sentence.
- Accepted limits; Proposed CHANGELOG line — Irreversibility of retirement for a late resume is not stated. Rejected 3/3. D2 reuses `begin_retirement_authored` with `RetireReason::Closed`, the same terminal retirement the user performed by hand on the 15 laptop seats; Accepted limit 1 names the resumed-session loss; AC2 keeps a seat whose pane still exists reattachable. The CHANGELOG could say "retire" more explicitly; wording only.
- AC8; D3 'No new wire value' — AC8 does not require amending C2's "A new or unknown incarnation never retires" sentence. Rejected 3/3. AC8 reads "states the rule (C2 pane-close retirement) and the accepted limit in the same commit", so it names the limit, and directs the edit into the C2 section where that sentence lives (TRUST-POLICY.md line 237). The design retires on a later same-incarnation observation, not on the incarnation change, so the sentence is not contradicted.
