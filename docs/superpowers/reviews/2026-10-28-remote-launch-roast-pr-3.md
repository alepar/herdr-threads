## Rejected (with reason)
- src/service/peer_launcher.rs:359 — The new per-peer cap (MAX_RUNNING_PER_PEER = 4) counts every `peer_requests` row in state `running` without checking the in-memory `live` set, so rows whose worker exited without writing its outcome hold a slot until the next daemon restart and four of them lock that requesting node out with a false "N launches for that machine are already running here". Rejected 2-1 (reproduce REJECT FYI, refute REJECT FYI; ground CONFIRM Nit). Reason: the mechanism is undisputed (running_for is a plain COUNT at peer_requests.rs:169; Worker::record's comment says a row whose outcome writes all fail "stays `running`"), but the precondition is a persistent store-write outage that defeats 3 s budgets through every RECORD_RETRIES attempt, four separate times for one peer, with no repeat of those ids and the lost() write failing too; the same outage breaks the INSERT in decide() and most daemon work, so the cap is not the cause of the outage. The design accepts the stuck-row state (comment: "a repeat answers it unconfirmed"; the 2026-10-28 review notes on lost rows; N15's 7-day retention and restart reconciliation), the spec states the rule as "at most 4 running launches per requesting node", counting unknown-outcome rows is the conservative reading, and Unavailable is a retryable answer. The ground seat's Nit concedes it is "rare and low-impact" and cites nothing the majority does not address. Carried as a note: subtracting rows absent from `live` when the cap is hit would be a cheap hardening, at the cost of not counting a pane that may have launched.
